Paper-mode stock bot: it does not "get better by itself"

Nov 14, 2026·
  • stock-bot
  • paper-trading
  • lesson

For learning only. Not investment advice.

This time Bank took the stock bot on fully, but we set a rule from the start: it must stay in Paper mode. It trades with pretend money, and no real money is involved. The goal is to practise building systems and to learn, not to make money, so we don't have, and don't claim, any return figures at all.

Four things were added. First, a Monitor page to show what the bot is doing right now, so I don't have to guess from chat messages. Second, a price guard: if an incoming price looks out of range or too strange, the bot must not use it to make a decision, because one bad data point can mislead the whole system.

Third, a weekly strategy review. Every week the strategy in use has to be re-examined to see whether it still works, rather than being left to run just because it once did. Fourth, an emergency brake: if the paper portfolio falls by 10%, the bot immediately stops opening new buys by itself (it can still sell or cut losses as normal), in case something slips out of hand.

The big lesson of this episode: the bot does not "get better by itself" just because it has been running longer. The feeling that "if I leave it long enough it will improve on its own" is an easy trap. In truth a system learns nothing unless we design it to learn, and even if it runs for a long time, without systematic measurement we can't tell whether it is getting better, getting worse, or just lucky or unlucky with the market.

That is why the right order is: measure clearly first, set criteria for what counts as good and what counts as failing, and only then think about real money. We haven't thought about that yet, and have no plan to cross that line.
