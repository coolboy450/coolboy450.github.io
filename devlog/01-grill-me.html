Letting an AI interview me until we had a team and a brand guide

Nov 13, 2026·
  • grill-me
  • brand
  • ai-team

At first we only had a vague idea: a small team of helpers for building websites and bots. But when I sat down to think about it, I couldn't answer a single question. Who does what? What should the brand look like? Which work needs a second pair of eyes? These questions kept circling in my head and never got written down anywhere.

So we tried a skill called grill-me. The idea is simple: the AI asks the questions, one at a time, instead of me giving the orders. Only when one is answered does it move on to the next, so you never get ten questions thrown at you at once. The upside is that you have to think about each thing properly. You can answer "whatever works", but the AI will ask what that means, and in the end you can't dodge it.

After enough answers, two things came out of it. First, a team of four AI roles: a UI designer, a frontend developer, a graphic designer, and a brand keeper who also reviews the work. Each role has clear limits on what it may and may not do. For example, the one writing code may not change the design direction, and the reviewer has to follow a checklist rather than go by mood.

Second, the noob_dev brand guide. The big picture is "an indie developer who stays a beginner, forever". The tone is friendly, short, direct and unpretentious. The main colours are a dark theme with a lime green, the font is IBM Plex, and there are small rules, like the _ in the logo always being green because it is the cursor waiting for you to type more, which suits someone who is always learning.

What I like most is that the guide did not come from sitting down to write a long document. It came from answering questions one by one, so the rules actually fit me instead of being pretty rules borrowed from somewhere else. And once the guide exists, everyone on the team has one source of truth to open, so nobody has to guess whether a colour is allowed or how much spacing to use.

The lesson of this episode is short: if you can't say what you want, flip it around. Let the AI do the asking and you do the answering. Sometimes the answer is already in your head and nobody has asked at the right moment. The next episode shows how this team ended up in a pixel office.
