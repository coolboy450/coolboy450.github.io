EP.13

Three drafts and the first Discord ping

Nov 17, 2026·
  • video-editor
  • drafts
  • discord

With the Publish button in place, I wanted a real job to push through the whole pipeline. I have a video editor project, so the brief was a browser video editor that actually works: a media library, a preview with playback and speed, a multi-track timeline with cut and delete, colour adjustments and filters, text on video, undo and redo, autosave, and export. It runs entirely in the browser, so nothing a user loads leaves their machine.

The brief also had rules that come straight from earlier episodes: no form that pretends to send data, no button for a feature that doesn't work, no e-mail addresses or server details in the files, and an honest list of what works and what doesn't at the end.

Mint came back with three drafts. A, "editing desk": four panes like a normal editor, so the library, preview, settings and timeline are all visible at once, folding into three tabs on a phone. B, "small cinema": a big preview with tools in drawers and a film-strip timeline, aimed at beginners. C, "noob console": a terminal look with a half-screen timeline and a shortcut bar, for people who cut with the keyboard.

This was also the first time the Discord notice fired for a real job: "waiting for you to choose a draft" arrived on my phone. That was the part I had not been able to test before.

I picked A. B was the prettiest, but a film strip fights against several tracks of video, audio and text, and hiding tools in drawers works against a brief that needs all of them. C fits the brand, but only suits keyboard users. A is the plainest, and it is the one that fits what we asked for. The plan was to borrow C's shortcut bar in a note, but I pressed send before typing it.

The lesson: drafts are cheap, so look at all of them, then pick the one that fits the brief, not the one that looks best in a screenshot.
