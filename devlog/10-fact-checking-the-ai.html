EP.10

Fact-checking what the AI wrote about us

Nov 17, 2026·
  • website
  • ai-team
  • lesson

For the main site, the team also wrote the first five devlog posts. I gave them a list of what really happened, told them not to invent numbers or events, and they came back with polished, readable posts. It would have been easy to publish them as they were.

Instead I read each post against what we had actually done, and found plain mistakes. The team's roles had been rewritten in a more flattering way: the graphic designer had become "the creative", the reviewer "the team mentor", and Bank, the trading-bot developer, "the budget keeper". These slipped into the posts, the team page and the home page. The emergency brake had turned into "a button that stops everything", when it really only stops new buys once the paper portfolio drops 10%. And one post quoted a line from the chat, something about someone changing a colour, that nobody had actually said.

None of this was malicious. When a model has gaps to fill, it fills them with sentences that sound right. The automated review looked at contrast, spacing and line height, which are things it can measure, and didn't notice, because it had no way to know what really happened.

We corrected every one of them, and replaced the invented quote with something that did happen: a team member drinking a bubble tea that had been reserved for someone else.

The lesson: give the AI the facts, tell it not to invent, and then still check. The more polished the writing, the easier it is to trust, and the more it needs a read-through by someone who was there.
