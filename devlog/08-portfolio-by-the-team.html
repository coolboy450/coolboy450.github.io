EP.08

A portfolio site built by the team, finished by hand

Nov 14, 2026·
  • website
  • review
  • github-pages

I wanted a portfolio site, so I asked the team to build one in the studio. Mint (the UI designer) came back with three drafts: a single terminal window, a split dock with project cards, and a tmux-style tab bar.

Looking at the real drafts changed my mind about what I'd assumed from the descriptions. The split dock had an empty "screenshot" box on every project card, and we had no real screenshots yet, so it would look unfinished. The tmux version's tab bar overflowed at phone width. I picked the single window, and borrowed the row of pixel characters from the tmux draft.

Bit built it, and Kru Waen reviewed it against the brand checklist. The review bounced three small things: Thai text in the footer used the monospace font, which has no Thai letters, so the browser quietly swapped in another font; the glasses on Kru Waen's sprite were almost the same colour as the face (a contrast of about 1.4 to 1); and Pop's hair and Bank's shirt were the same colour as their faces, which turned the characters into blobs.

The studio allows two automatic fix rounds. After that it hands the decision to the owner, and the review was still marked "not passed". The notes were precise, though, down to the line and the replacement value, so rather than send the team around again we approved it and fixed the three things by hand.

We checked the new colours with a contrast calculation instead of by eye: the glasses came out at 11.3 to 1 in the dark theme and 4.8 to 1 in the light one, and the hair and shirts now differ from the faces in both themes. Then the site went live on GitHub Pages, for free, after a scan for anything private: no server addresses, no email, no internal links.

The lesson: an automatic review loop is great for catching small things, but it stops after a few rounds. When the remaining issues are precise, a human finishing them is faster than another lap.
